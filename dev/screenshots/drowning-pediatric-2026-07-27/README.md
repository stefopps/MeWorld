## Hook

You walk into a resuscitation bay. A child has been pulled from water. She is not breathing. She has a weak pulse. She is blue. Her skin is cold. The monitor shows bradycardia below 60. This is not a "wait and see" moment. Every second of hypoxia is killing neurons. You need to act before you think. The clock started when she went under.

---

## Mechanism — Why Drowning Kills

### The Pathophysiology Chain

1. **Submersion → breath-holding → laryngospasm.** The first thing that happens is not aspiration. It's involuntary breath-holding followed by laryngospasm. The glottis slams shut. For 30-90 seconds, no water enters the lungs. But no air enters either. Oxygen falls. CO₂ rises.

2. **Hypoxic loss of consciousness → laryngospasm breaks.** When the brainstem can no longer sustain the spasm, the glottis relaxes. Water enters the airway. The patient aspirates.

3. **Aspiration → surfactant washout → alveolar collapse.** Water in the alveoli dilutes and washes out surfactant. Without surfactant, alveoli collapse at end-expiration. The lung stiffens. Compliance drops. Ventilation-perfusion mismatch explodes. This is ARDS in fast-forward.

4. **Hypoxemia → bradycardia → cardiac arrest.** The heart of a child is oxygen-dependent. An adult heart can run on its own reserves for a few minutes. A child's heart cannot. When PaO₂ drops below about 30 mmHg, the myocardium fails. Bradycardia is the pre-terminal rhythm. Asystole follows. This is why PALS says: if the heart rate is below 60 with poor perfusion, start CPR. You do not wait for asystole. Bradycardia in a hypoxic child IS a cardiac arrest.

5. **Cold water → hypothermia → cold diuresis → volume depletion.** Cold water immersion triggers peripheral vasoconstriction, shunting blood centrally. The hypothalamus perceives this as volume overload — atrial stretch receptors fire. ADH release is suppressed. The kidneys dump water. The patient becomes hypovolemic despite drowning.

6. **Pulmonary edema on CXR.** The "drowning lung" on chest X-ray shows diffuse, homogeneous infiltrates. Lung markings blur. Heart borders disappear into the edema. This is direct alveolar injury from aspirated water combined with surfactant dysfunction. It is indistinguishable from ARDS on imaging.

## The PALS Algorithm for Drowning

| Step | Action | Why |
|------|--------|-----|
| 1 | Remove from water. Remove cold wet clothing. | Cold clothes worsen hypothermia. Hypothermia worsens bradycardia. |
| 2 | Check pulse. If HR <60 with poor perfusion: start CPR. | PALS: bradycardia with poor perfusion IS an arrest in a child. |
| 3 | Intubate. Mechanical ventilation. 100% oxygen. | Airway protection. Positive pressure opens collapsed alveoli. |
| 4 | Cardiac monitor + pulse oximetry. | You need to know the rhythm and the oxygen. |
| 5 | ABG. | Objective measurement of oxygenation, ventilation, and acid-base status. |
| 6 | Warm IV fluids (NS or LR). | Cold diuresis → volume depletion. Warm fluids contribute to rewarming. |
| 7 | Warming blanket. | Passive external rewarming. Core temperature must rise. |
| 8 | Epinephrine per PALS. | Restores cardiac output in the bradycardic hypoxic heart. |
| 9 | Transfer to ICU. | Post-arrest care. Neuroprotection. Continued rewarming. |

## First Principles: The Dumb Mistakes and Why They Happen

You said BP was 30/25. You fixed the pressure once but not the heart. Here is what happened:

When a child is hypoxic, the myocardium fails. Epinephrine is the drug that forces it to keep contracting — alpha agonism squeezes the vasculature to raise pressure, beta agonism drives the heart rate up. Without epinephrine, the pressure you raised with fluids collapses again because the pump is still failing.

The cardiac monitor is equally critical. Without it, you cannot know if the child has slipped into ventricular fibrillation — which CPR can actually induce in a hypothermic, hypoxic heart. You need to see the rhythm to guide the next intervention.

**The pattern:** You stabilized the patient (fluids, warming, intubation) but you stopped short of the cardiac-specific interventions (epinephrine, monitor, CPR). The goal is not just "fix the pressure." The goal is "restore oxygen delivery to the brain by restoring the pump." Every step that does not directly address that goal is secondary.

---

## The Attending's Law for Pediatric Arrest

> A hypoxic child does not arrest the way an adult arrests. Adults arrest from arrhythmia. Children arrest from respiratory failure. Treat the hypoxia first. But do not forget that the failing heart needs epinephrine. Oxygen without circulation is useless. Circulation without oxygen is useless. You need both.
