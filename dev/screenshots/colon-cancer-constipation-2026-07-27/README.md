# Colon Adenocarcinoma — Attending Walkthrough

**Date:** 2026-07-27 | **Score:** 41.67% (Avg 75.7%) | **Patient:** 71M, constipation, nausea, fatigue

## Hook

You were thinking diverticulosis. That was the right neighborhood -- a 71-year-old with constipation and diffuse abdominal tenderness. But the physical exam handed you the answer: stool occult blood was positive, and the liver was palpable and enlarged. That combination in a 71-year-old is colon cancer with liver metastases until proven otherwise.

Your CT was the right call. You found the mass. You staged. But you stopped at diagnosis. A cancer diagnosis without a surgical plan and pre-op clearance is a diagnosis you watched grow -- same pattern as the hypothyroidism case earlier today.

## Mechanism: Why Left-Sided Colon Cancer Behaves Differently

The colon is not one organ. The left colon (descending, sigmoid) has a narrow lumen and formed stool. The right colon (ascending, cecum) has a wide lumen and liquid stool.

| Location | Symptoms | Why |
|----------|----------|-----|
| **Left-sided** | Obstruction, constipation, decreased stool caliber, colicky pain | Narrow lumen + formed stool = mass blocks the pipe early |
| **Right-sided** | Iron deficiency anemia, fatigue, weight loss, occult bleeding | Wide lumen + liquid stool = mass grows silently, bleeds into stool without obstruction |

This patient had constipation and decreased stool caliber. Left-sided.

The hepatomegaly on exam? Portal drainage of the colon goes straight to the liver via the portal vein. Colon cancer metastasizes to the liver first -- it's not random, it's portal anatomy.

## What You Got Right

CT abdomen/pelvis with contrast -- correct. CT is the gold standard for colorectal cancer staging. CBC and CMP were appropriate baseline labs. Rectal exam caught the occult blood.

## What You Missed

| Missed | Impact |
|--------|--------|
| **Colonoscopy** | Biopsy confirmation. Sigmoidoscopy doesn't cover enough colon -- you need the full scope before surgery. |
| **CEA level** | Tumor marker. Pre-op baseline determines prognosis. Post-op trend detects recurrence. CEA < 2-3 post-op = good prognosis. |
| **ECG** | Pre-op cardiac clearance. 71-year-old heading to bowel resection. |
| **PT/PTT + type & screen** | Bleeding risk + blood availability before surgery. |
| **NPO** | Bowel prep. Nothing by mouth before abdominal surgery. |
| **Surgical consult** | Resection. Then chemo if nodes positive. |
| **Cancer counseling** | Tell the patient. Standard preventive care on CCS. |
