## Hook

He did not tear a muscle. He did not clot a vein. He grew bacteria in the soft tissue of a leg that has been bathing in high sugar for years. The puzzle is not "red leg equals cellulitis." The puzzle is why *this* diabetic gets invasive gram-positive soft tissue disease, why duplex still matters, and why antibiotics alone do not finish the case.

---

## Mechanism: Sugar Breaks the Door; Bugs Walk the Plane

Picture the skin as a living wall. Keratinocytes, tight junctions, and a neutrophil patrol keep streptococci and staphylococci outside.

Chronic hyperglycemia dissolves that wall from the inside. Neutrophil chemotaxis slows. Complement opsonization dulls. Microabrasions that a euglycemic host would seal overnight stay open. Bacteria enter the dermis and subcutis and spread *laterally* through loose connective tissue. That is why the erythema is poorly demarcated — not a crisp abscess pocket, not a surgical necrotizing edge, but a diffuse warm plane of infection.

**Spatial logic.** Unilateral. Anterior calf. Hot. Indurated. No ulcer, no drainable pus. Elevation helps because dependent edema amplifies stretch pain; walking worsens it because you are pumping inflamed tissue. Fever 38.3 C and WBC 11.3k say the cytokine alarm has gone systemic. This is not "watch and wait outpatient cream" territory.

**Contrast — DVT.** Same unilateral swollen red leg story. Different physics: obstructed venous outflow, not dermal bacterial invasion. You ordered duplex. No DVT. That single negative is what lets you commit to cellulitis instead of anticoagulation. Keep that move.

**Connecting thread — the glucose.** Infection is a catecholamine/cortisol storm. Hepatic glucose output rises. Metformin, still on his list, does not rescue acute stress hyperglycemia. High sugar further cripples the same neutrophils trying to clear the bug. The infection and the glucose are one loop, not two problems.

## Clinical Anchors

**Blood cultures before (or with) empiric abx** — febrile soft tissue infection can seed blood. You got this.

**Empiric gram-positive coverage** — MSSA and beta-hemolytic strep dominate non-purulent cellulitis. Clindamycin counted. Systemic signs → IV, inpatient.

**A1c is not paperwork** — it is the receipt for months of barrier failure that invited this admission.

**Location is a treatment order** — ED at sim 00:15 and 15:15 when the correct place was Inpatient Unit. IV antibiotics live on the floor.
