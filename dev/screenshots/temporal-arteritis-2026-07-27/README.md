## Mechanism

Giant cell arteritis is a T-cell-mediated granulomatous vasculitis targeting medium-to-large arteries — especially the cranial branches of the aorta.

CD4+ T cells infiltrate the adventitia of the temporal artery. They recruit macrophages. The macrophages fuse into multinucleated giant cells at the intima-media junction. These cells release IL-6, TNF-alpha, and matrix metalloproteinases. The internal elastic lamina fragments. The intima thickens dramatically in response (intimal hyperplasia). The lumen narrows. Segmental "skip lesions" mean only parts of the vessel are affected.

**Why vision is threatened:** The ophthalmic artery branches from the internal carotid. The posterior ciliary arteries supply the optic nerve head. When GCA inflames these arteries, the optic nerve loses blood supply — anterior ischemic optic neuropathy (AION). This is the most common cause of permanent visual loss in GCA. It can happen in hours.

**Why jaw claudication:** The facial artery supplies the masseter. Inflamed → can't deliver blood during chewing → muscle cramps → pain with mastication. Classic GCA symptom.

**Why ESR and CRP are almost always elevated:** IL-6 drives the liver to produce acute phase reactants. Fibrinogen elevates ESR. CRP is produced directly. ESR is often >100 in active GCA. A normal ESR makes GCA very unlikely. These are not specific tests — any inflammation raises them — but they are exquisitely sensitive.

**Why prednisone, not dexamethasone:** Prednisone has an intermediate duration of action (12-36 hours). Dexamethasone is a long-acting fluorinated steroid (36-54 hours) that accumulates. More adrenal suppression, more osteoporosis, more myopathy with prolonged use. For a disease requiring 12-18 months of treatment with slow tapering, prednisone's pharmacokinetics are vastly superior. When visual symptoms are present, guidelines recommend IV methylprednisolone days 1-3, then transition to oral prednisone 60-80 mg/day.

**Associated aortitis:** GCA is a systemic large-vessel vasculitis. The thoracic aorta can be involved. Patients have a 17-fold increased risk of thoracic aortic aneurysm. Screen with chest imaging at diagnosis.
