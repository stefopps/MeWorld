3x3 grid, 9 panels -- what was missed in an unstable angina case. Patient got aspirin and nitroglycerin but the full ACS bundle was never completed. Cardiology was consulted but catheterization never ordered. Patient stayed in ED worsening.

Camera: medium, close, medium, medium, close, wide, medium, medium, wide.

Panel 1 (DAPT -- TWO DRUGS): Pill cup on bedside table. Two pills visible: aspirin (white) and clopidogrel (pink). Second pill emphasized -- glowing slightly, critical. Aspirin alone blocks COX-1. P2Y12 inhibitor blocks ADP receptor -- the OTHER platelet pathway. One antiplatelet is half the strategy. Platelets can still aggregate through ADP. DAPT is standard of care for all NSTE-ACS.

Panel 2 (ANTICOAGULATION -- SECOND LAYER): IV pole. Heparin drip set up -- bag hanging, tubing connected to patient's arm. Antiplatelets block platelets. Anticoagulation blocks thrombin and factor Xa -- the coagulation cascade. Separate systems. Both activated in ACS. Both must be suppressed. Without heparin, the thrombus continues forming fibrin.

Panel 3 (BETA-BLOCKER -- OXYGEN DEMAND): Nurse administering IV metoprolol. Monitor showing HR 90 dropping to 68 as drug takes effect. Beta-1 blockade reduces heart rate and contractility. Myocardial oxygen demand drops. The ischemic subendocardium gets more time in diastole -- the only time it's perfused. Standard of care in ACS. No contraindications present.

Panel 4 (STATIN -- PLAQUE STABILIZATION): Pharmacy counter. Atorvastatin 80mg being dispensed. High-intensity statin. All ACS patients get this regardless of baseline LDL. Plaque stabilization, reduced inflammation, lipid core shrinkage, fibrous cap thickening. This is not a wait-for-labs drug. This is an immediate-intervention drug.

Panel 5 (CATHETERIZATION -- THE TREATMENT): Cath lab in action. Surgeon at femoral access. Fluoroscopy monitor showing LAD stenosis. The cardiology consult replied: "Cardiology will see the patient. Please order any urgent interventions." The consult is a message. The catheterization is the answer. The stent is what saves myocardium. Ordering the consult without ordering the procedure is like calling a plumber and not letting him fix the leak.

Panel 6 (TRANSFER -- ICU): Patient being wheeled from ED bay to ICU. Six hours have passed. Chest pain persists -- "someone is standing on my chest." Patient is worsening, not improving. ED cannot provide the level of monitoring and care needed. ICU: cardiac monitor, nurse ratios, rapid response, ability to titrate drips. Location matters.

Panel 7 (PREVENTIVE GAPS -- MISSED): Empty chair in clinic. Cigarette pack on desk. Fast food container. No exercise plan. No cardiac rehab referral. No low-salt counseling. No vaccines. No ACE-I for untreated hypertension. 0% on preventive care. The plaque that fissured today was fed by decades of neglect. The future plaques are being fed right now.

Panel 8 (MONITORING + LABS -- SAFETY): Cardiac monitor screen showing sinus rhythm. Next to it: lab requisitions for Mg, lipid profile, HbA1c, PT/PTT. Cardiac patients need continuous rhythm monitoring -- VF/VT kills in minutes without warning. Magnesium deficiency causes torsades. HbA1c guides diabetes management. PT/PTT needed before arterial access for cath. These are safety nets, not optional.

Panel 9 (LONG GAME -- CLINIC VICTORY): Outpatient clinic. Patient healthy, engaged. Prescription bottles: aspirin, clopidogrel, atorvastatin, metoprolol, lisinopril. No cigarettes. Exercise shoes visible. Appointment card for cardiac rehab. Tdap and Shingrix vaccine records on chart. The complete ACS bundle -- acute management PLUS secondary prevention. Survived the event. Surviving the future.
