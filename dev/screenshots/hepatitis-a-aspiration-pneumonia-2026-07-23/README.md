# Hepatitis A with Aspiration Pneumonia — Attending Walkthrough

**Date:** 2026-07-23 | **Score:** 63.8% (Avg 62.1%) | **Patient:** 35-year-old male, accountant

---

## Hook

He just got back from a Caribbean cruise. He was drinking — not heavily, but more than usual. On the trip, he started vomiting. The ship made an emergency stop on an island. They gave him an antiemetic and sent him back. His skin turned yellow on the flight home. His wife drove him to the ED while he vomited into the car — and aspirated.

AST 3117. ALT 2362. Total bilirubin 23.7. Hepatitis A IgM positive. Fetor hepaticus. And now, right lower lobe aspiration pneumonia from the car ride.

Two diseases, one patient. The hepatitis is the fire. The aspiration pneumonia is the smoke. But you have to fight both at once.

---

## Mechanism: What the Liver Does

The liver has 500 functions. When it fails, only two matter acutely:

**1. Detoxification.** The liver clears ammonia, bilirubin, drugs, and toxins from the portal circulation. When hepatocytes die, ammonia accumulates. Ammonia crosses the blood-brain barrier and impairs astrocyte function. Neurons can't maintain their resting potential. Confusion, asterixis, then coma. This is hepatic encephalopathy.

**2. Synthetic function.** The liver makes albumin, clotting factors (II, VII, IX, X — vitamin K-dependent), and complement. When synthesis fails, albumin drops → third-spacing and edema. Clotting factors drop → coagulopathy. INR rises. The patient can bleed from anywhere.

**AST 3117, ALT 2362** tell you the degree of hepatocyte necrosis. These enzymes live inside hepatocytes. When the cell membrane ruptures, they spill into the blood. The higher the number, the more cells dying per minute. These are not "slightly elevated." These are "the liver is being dismantled in real time."

**Total bilirubin 23.7** tells you the liver has lost its ability to conjugate and excrete bilirubin. Direct bilirubin is high because the hepatocytes can't pump it into bile. Indirect bilirubin is high because the surviving hepatocytes are overwhelmed. Jaundice at 23.7 is deep — the sclerae are mustard, the skin is saffron. This patient looks like a highlighter.

## Spatial Logic: Fetor Hepaticus

The foul breath. "Putrid," his wife said. This is not poor hygiene. It's portal-systemic shunting.

Normally, the liver clears sulfur-containing compounds (mercaptans) from portal blood. When the liver fails, those compounds bypass the liver through portosystemic collaterals, enter the systemic circulation, and are exhaled through the lungs. The breath smells like a mixture of rotten eggs and garlic — dimethyl sulfide and methyl mercaptan.

Fetor hepaticus is a physical exam finding that tells you the liver's detoxification capacity is critically impaired. It appears before asterixis. It's a warning sign.

---

## Contrast: Hepatitis A vs Acetaminophen Toxicity

AST 3117 and ALT 2362 in a young person with jaundice is either viral hepatitis or acetaminophen overdose. You must rule out APAP. If the level is positive, N-acetylcysteine (NAC) is life-saving. NAC repletes glutathione — the liver's antioxidant shield — and directly scavenges the toxic NAPQI metabolite.

Hepatitis A is fecal-oral transmission. Contaminated food or water. Cruise ships are perfect vectors: shared food preparation, close quarters, variable sanitation at ports. The incubation period is 15-50 days. He likely contracted it early in the cruise. Symptoms appeared 7 days before presentation — right on schedule.

The key differentiator: APAP toxicity gives you AST and ALT in the thousands (1,000-10,000+) with AST > ALT early, and the bilirubin rises later. Viral hepatitis gives you ALT > AST (the classic hepatocellular pattern) with bilirubin rising alongside the transaminases. This patient had hepatitis A IgM positive. That's the answer. But you always check APAP first — it's the one you can reverse.

## Connecting Thread: The Aspiration

He was vomiting uncontrollably in the car. Vomit entered his airway. Gastric acid is pH 1-2. It chemically burns the tracheobronchial tree and alveolar epithelium. The injury is two-phase: chemical pneumonitis in the first hours (acid burn, not infection), then bacterial superinfection 48-72 hours later (aspiration pneumonia).

The CXR showed right lower lobe consolidation. Aspiration in a seated or semi-recumbent patient goes to the right lower lobe — gravity, straight shot down the right mainstem bronchus.

He needs broad-spectrum antibiotics covering anaerobes and gram-negatives. Piperacillin/tazobactam or ceftriaxone + metronidazole. Oxygen. Monitoring for ARDS.

---

## Clinical Anchors

**Supportive care is the mainstay for hepatitis A.** No antiviral. No steroid. The liver heals itself — acute hepatitis A has a >99% survival rate in healthy adults. But "supportive" means vigilant: monitor INR, albumin, glucose, mental status. Antiemetics. IV fluids. Correct electrolytes — his sodium was 129 and potassium was 3.0 from vomiting.

**No alcohol. Ever again.** At least until liver enzymes normalize. Alcohol is hepatotoxic. A liver struggling to regenerate after viral assault cannot handle ethanol metabolism. The patient was a social drinker — this conversation needs to happen.

**Octreotide is not indicated here.** Octreotide is for variceal bleeding — it reduces splanchnic blood flow and portal pressure. Hepatitis A does not cause varices. This was an unnecessary order.
