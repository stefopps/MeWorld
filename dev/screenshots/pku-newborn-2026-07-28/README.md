## Mechanism: The Blocked Enzyme

Picture phenylalanine hydroxylase (PAH) sitting in the liver. It takes phenylalanine — an essential amino acid from dietary protein — and adds a hydroxyl group to the benzene ring, converting it to tyrosine. This reaction needs tetrahydrobiopterin (BH4) as a cofactor and molecular oxygen. It is the only way out for phenylalanine. There is no backup pathway.

In classic PKU, the PAH gene on chromosome 12 has a mutation — usually a missense mutation that reduces enzyme activity to less than 1% of normal. Phenylalanine arrives from the diet and cannot leave. It accumulates. Serum levels climb from normal (<2 mg/dL) to 20+ mg/dL.

Three things happen with that accumulation:

**1. Brain toxicity.** Phenylalanine competes with other large neutral amino acids (tyrosine, tryptophan) for the LAT1 transporter at the blood-brain barrier. High phenylalanine outcompetes them. The brain becomes starved of tyrosine (needed for dopamine, norepinephrine, epinephrine synthesis) and tryptophan (needed for serotonin). Myelination fails. White matter lesions form. IQ drops — irreversible if untreated in the first weeks of life. Every day of delay costs measurable IQ points.

**2. Melanin deficiency.** Tyrosine is the precursor for melanin. When PAH is blocked, tyrosine production falls. Melanocytes in skin, hair, and iris cannot make pigment. Untreated PKU children develop fair skin, blonde hair, and blue eyes — regardless of genetic background. This patient is only 3 weeks old, so not enough time has passed for this manifestation. But it is coming.

**3. Musty odor.** Phenylalanine accumulates to the point where alternative metabolic pathways kick in. Phenylalanine transaminase converts it to phenylpyruvic acid, which decarboxylates to phenylacetic acid. Phenylacetic acid is volatile. It is excreted in sweat and urine. It smells musty, mousy — the pathognomonic odor of PKU. The mother notices it in the diaper. That one observation is worth more than the entire meningitis workup you ordered.

**The clinical anchor:** A newborn with irritability, eczema, and musty-smelling urine = PKU until proven otherwise. One blood test. One dietary change.

## The Trap: Why PKU Is Called Phenylketonuria

The name ends in "-uria," so your instinct is to check the urine. But phenylketonuria was named in 1934 by Dr. Ivar Følling, who found abnormal ketones (phenylpyruvic acid) in the urine of intellectually disabled children. The ketones turned dark green with ferric chloride — a different reaction from the usual red-brown of acetoacetate.

The trap: standard UA ketones test for acetoacetic acid and beta-hydroxybutyrate using the nitroprusside reaction. Phenylpyruvic acid does NOT react with nitroprusside. So a standard UA will show "ketones negative" even though the patient has ketoaciduria.

**PKU is diagnosed in blood, not urine.** Serum phenylalanine. Spot test. One tube. Done.

Another trap: the Guthrie test (newborn screening). In the US, every newborn is screened for PKU at 24-72 hours of life via heel prick. This case only presents because the infant was born outside the US or lost to follow-up. In the real world, you will never see an undiagnosed 3-week-old with PKU in America — newborn screening catches it. The CCS case is testing whether you know what PKU looks like without the screening crutch.

## First Principles

**Why phenylalanine kills brain cells but not liver cells:** The brain imports amino acids via the LAT1 transporter, which has equal affinity for all large neutral amino acids. When phenylalanine is 10x normal, it saturates LAT1 and blocks tyrosine/tryptophan entry. The liver does not use LAT1 — it uses system A and system N transporters with different specificity. The brain starves while the liver is fine.

**Why eczema develops in PKU:** Phenylalanine is excreted in sweat. On the skin surface, it crystallizes and acts as a direct irritant. It is not an allergic eczema — it is a chemical irritant dermatitis from amino acid crystals. This is why it appears in the first weeks of life, before any dietary diversity, and resolves with phenylalanine restriction.

**Why treatment is urgent and irreversible if delayed:** Myelination peaks in the first 2 years of life. Phenylalanine toxicity during this window causes permanent white matter damage. Starting the diet at 3 weeks preserves normal IQ. Starting at 3 months loses 10-15 IQ points. Starting at 3 years loses 30+ points — and they never come back. Every day matters.
