## Mechanism — Chlamydia as an Obligate Intracellular Pathogen

Chlamydia trachomatis cannot make its own ATP. It is an **energy parasite** — it imports ATP from the host cell through a unique ADP/ATP translocase. This is why it must live inside host epithelial cells (urethral, cervical, conjunctival).

**Life cycle:**
1. **Elementary body (EB):** The infectious form. Metabolically inert, tough cell wall. Attaches to host epithelium → endocytosed.
2. **Reticulate body (RB):** Inside the inclusion vacuole, the EB reorganizes into the RB — metabolically active, replicating by binary fission.
3. **Elementary body reformation:** RBs condense back into EBs → host cell lyses → EBs released to infect neighboring cells.

Azithromycin binds the **50S ribosomal subunit** and blocks translocation of the growing peptide chain. Doxycycline binds the **30S subunit** and blocks tRNA entry. Both halt protein synthesis — chlamydia cannot replicate without host ATP or its own proteins.

## Why the UA Can Be Negative in Urethritis

This is a classic CCS trap. Urethritis vs cystitis:

| Finding | Cystitis (bladder) | Urethritis (urethra) |
|---------|-------------------|---------------------|
| **Leukocyte esterase** | Positive | Often negative |
| **Nitrites** | Often positive (Gram-negatives) | Negative |
| **WBCs on microscopy** | Present | May be present only in first-catch |
| **Diagnosis** | Urine culture | NAAT (first-catch urine or urethral swab) |

The dipstick was negative because the infection is in the urethra, not the bladder. The purulent discharge is coming from the urethral mucosa, bypassing the bladder. If you rely on a negative UA to rule out infection, you miss urethritis.

## The STI Bundle — What CCS Wants After Diagnosis

| Intervention | Why |
|---|---|
| **HIV screen** | One STI = screen for all. HIV co-infection amplifies transmission risk. |
| **Hepatitis B/C screen** | Sexual transmission of HBV/HCV. Vaccinate if susceptible. |
| **Partner notification + treatment** | Reinfection rate is near 100% if untreated partner. Expedited partner therapy (EPT) is legal in most states. |
| **No intercourse x 7 days** | CDC guideline. Until azithromycin course complete AND partners treated. |
| **Safe sex counseling** | Condom use, risk reduction. STI visit is the highest-yield moment for this conversation. |
| **Smoking cessation** | Patient smokes. Every visit is a tobacco intervention opportunity. |
| **Illicit drug counseling** | Patient admitted to substance use. Must address. |

Preventive care was only 5% of the total score, so the 0% there cost ~5 points. The diagnosis and treatment scores were solid (82% and 83%). Combined, this lands at 81%.

## Chlamydia-Gonorrhea Co-Infection Rule

- **40% of women** and **20% of men** with chlamydia also have gonorrhea
- Always co-test (NAAT for both from a single urine specimen)
- Always empirically cover both: **azithromycin + ceftriaxone** (or cefixime oral)
- You did this correctly — cefixime for gonorrhea, azithromycin for chlamydia
