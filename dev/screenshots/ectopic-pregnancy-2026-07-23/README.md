## Hook

Infertility is not sterility. Every reproductive-age woman with pelvic pain is ectopic until proven otherwise. She needed surgery. She didn't get it. The case timed out while she was bleeding into her peritoneal cavity.

The diagnosis was right. The treatment never happened.

---

## Mechanism: Why the Tube Ruptures

A fertilized ovum is supposed to implant in the endometrium — a thick, vascular, distensible tissue designed to accommodate a growing embryo for nine months.

In an ectopic pregnancy, the ovum implants in the fallopian tube. The tube is not the uterus. It's a narrow muscular conduit. Its wall is thin. It cannot distend. As the trophoblast invades the tubal mucosa and then the muscularis, it erodes into the tubal blood vessels. The tube stretches. Then it tears.

When the tube ruptures, maternal blood spills directly into the peritoneal cavity. There is no tamponade — the peritoneal space is open. The bleeding continues until the pressure drops enough to slow the flow, or until a surgeon ties off the bleeding vessel.

The blood itself is an irritant. The peritoneum registers it as a chemical burn. That's where rigidity, guarding, and rebound come from — not infection, not inflammation. Just free blood, chemically irritating the most sensitive membrane in the body.

---

## Spatial Logic: The Hemorrhagic Shock Sequence

**Tachycardia comes first.** The heart detects falling preload — less blood returning because it's pooling in the belly instead of circulating. The compensatory response is rate: HR 110. This is Class II hemorrhagic shock (750-1500 mL lost).

**Hypotension comes later.** When the compensatory mechanisms fail, the pressure drops. BP 70 systolic is Class III shock (1500-2000 mL lost). A 70 systolic in a young healthy woman means she has already lost 30-40% of her blood volume.

The clinical sequence is: peritoneal irritation → tachycardia → pallor → dropping pressure → altered mental status → cardiac arrest. She was at step 3 when the case ended.

---

## Contrast: The Dark Bloody Discharge

Ectopic pregnancies don't produce enough hCG to fully maintain the uterine lining. The decidua — the specialized endometrial tissue that forms in response to progesterone — breaks down and sheds. That's the dark bloody discharge from the cervix.

This is NOT the same as a spontaneous abortion. In a miscarriage, the uterus is cramping and passing products of conception through the cervix. In an ectopic, the uterus is empty. The discharge is decidual cast — the lining sloughing because the hormonal signal is too weak.

The combination of a positive pregnancy test, an empty uterus on ultrasound, vaginal bleeding, and an adnexal mass is the classic ectopic triad.

## Connecting Thread: Misguided Safety

She thinks she's infertile. Fertility medications were required for her first pregnancy. She assumes her body can't conceive on its own.

Infertility is not sterility. Infertility means reduced fecundity — it takes longer, or requires assistance, but pregnancy is still biologically possible. Every unprotected sexual encounter in a woman with ovaries and fallopian tubes carries a nonzero risk of pregnancy.

Add binge drinking — impaired judgment — and unprotected sex while intoxicated, and you have a pregnancy that the patient never considered possible. She didn't seek care early because she didn't think she could be pregnant. By the time she presented, the tube had already ruptured.

## Clinical Anchors

**Every reproductive-age woman with pelvic pain gets a pregnancy test.** Period. This is not negotiable. Even if she says she's infertile. Even if she has an IUD. Even if her tubes are tied (ectopic risk is higher after failed tubal ligation).

**Ectopic treatments are tiered by hemodynamic stability:**

| Patient | Treatment |
|---------|-----------|
| **Stable**, β-hCG <5,000, no fetal cardiac activity, mass <3.5 cm | **Methotrexate** (IM, single dose). Serial β-hCG until zero. |
| **Stable** but MTX contraindicated or failed | **Salpingostomy** (tube-preserving) or salpingectomy |
| **Unstable** — hypotension, tachycardia, peritoneal signs | **Emergency laparotomy/laparoscopy.** No MTX. No waiting. |

This patient was unstable. HR 110, BP 70, rigid abdomen with rebound. She needed the OR the moment the β-hCG came back positive and the US showed an empty uterus with free fluid. Not in 20 minutes. Not after the CBC resulted. Now.

**Type and screen before OR.** She needs blood available. If she's Rh-negative, she needs RhoGAM — any Rh-negative pregnant woman with vaginal bleeding gets Rhogam, regardless of whether the pregnancy is intrauterine or ectopic.

**NSAIDs are contraindicated.** She's hemorrhaging. Antiplatelet drugs make it worse. IV fentanyl or morphine only.

**Don't forget the preventive medicine.** Safe sex counseling. Smoking cessation — smoking is an independent risk factor for ectopic pregnancy (altered tubal motility). Alcohol counseling — binge drinking led to the unprotected sex. STI screening — undiagnosed chlamydia causes subclinical salpingitis and tubal damage. 0% on preventive care.
