## Mechanism: Why Herpes Zoster Causes Chronic Neuropathic Pain

### Varicella-zoster virus (VZV) does not leave the body after chickenpox.

After primary infection (chickenpox), VZV travels retrogradely along sensory nerves to the dorsal root ganglia and cranial nerve ganglia, where it enters latency. The viral genome persists as circular episomal DNA inside the neuronal cell body. The host immune system (specifically VZV-specific CD8+ T cells) keeps it suppressed for decades.

### Reactivation: the dermatomal march

When cell-mediated immunity wanes (age, stress, immunosuppression), VZV reactivates. It travels anterogradely down the sensory nerve to the skin, producing the characteristic dermatomal vesicular rash. The trigeminal ganglion is the most commonly affected cranial nerve ganglion, with the ophthalmic division (V1) most frequently involved.

### The pain comes from two distinct mechanisms

**Acute herpetic neuralgia (the rash phase):**
- Direct viral cytolysis of keratinocytes
- Inflammatory infiltrate in the dorsal root ganglion
- Hemorrhagic necrosis of the ganglion
- Nociceptor sensitization from local prostaglandins, bradykinin, cytokines

**Postherpetic neuralgia (PHN) - pain persisting >90 days after rash resolution:**
- **Deafferentation pain.** The virus destroys large myelinated A-beta fibers. These fibers normally gate pain transmission in the dorsal horn (Melzack and Wall's gate control theory). When A-beta fibers are lost, the gate stays open. C-fiber nociceptive input flows unimpeded to the thalamus.
- **Central sensitization.** Persistent nociceptive barrage upregulates NMDA receptors in the dorsal horn. The spinal cord becomes hyperexcitable. Non-painful stimuli (allodynia) now trigger pain.
- **Ectopic pacemakers.** Damaged nerve endings and the cell body in the ganglion develop spontaneous ectopic discharges. Sodium channels (Nav1.7, Nav1.8) accumulate at the injury site, lowering the threshold for spontaneous firing.

### This is why gabapentin/pregabalin works for PHN, and carbamazepine does not (first-line)

| Drug | Mechanism | Why right for PHN | Why wrong as first-line |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Gabapentin / Pregabalin** | Binds alpha-2-delta subunit of voltage-gated calcium channels. Reduces calcium influx into presynaptic terminals. Decreases release of glutamate, substance P, norepinephrine. **Dampens central sensitization by reducing excitatory neurotransmitter release in the dorsal horn.** | Targets the central sensitization mechanism directly. Reduces spontaneous ectopic firing. | Not a sodium channel blocker. Not for acute nerve firing. |
| **Carbamazepine / Oxcarbazepine** | Blocks voltage-gated sodium channels (Nav1.7). Stabilizes neuronal membranes. Prevents high-frequency repetitive firing. **Stops ephaptic transmission at vascular compression site.** | Right for classical trigeminal neuralgia where a blood vessel compresses the trigeminal root entry zone, causing demyelination and ephaptic cross-talk between touch and pain fibers. | PHN is not an ephaptic problem. The sodium channels at the injury site are already upregulated; blocking them helps some, but central sensitization (the dominant mechanism in PHN) is calcium-channel mediated, not sodium-channel mediated. |
| **TCAs (amitriptyline, nortriptyline)** | Inhibit serotonin and norepinephrine reuptake. Block sodium channels. Antagonize NMDA receptors. | Second-line for PHN. Multimodal: enhances descending inhibitory pathways plus NMDA antagonism at sensitized dorsal horn. | Anticholinergic side effects limit use in elderly. |
| **Valproic acid** | Enhances GABAergic transmission. | Third-line. GABA enhancement can suppress central sensitization but less targeted than gabapentin. | Teratogenicity, hepatotoxicity, weight gain. |

### The key distinction: ephaptic transmission vs deafferentation + central sensitization

**Classical trigeminal neuralgia:** An artery (usually the superior cerebellar artery) compresses the trigeminal root entry zone. This causes focal demyelination. Touch fibers and pain fibers become adjacent and demyelinated at the compression point. A light touch triggers an ephaptic discharge (electrical cross-talk) into the pain pathway. This produces the characteristic lancinating, electric-shock pain triggered by light touch, wind, shaving, brushing teeth.

Carbamazepine works beautifully here. It blocks the sodium channels at the demyelinated compression site, preventing the high-frequency burst firing that drives the ephaptic cross-talk.

**Postherpetic neuralgia:** There is no vascular compression and no ephaptic cross-talk. The virus destroyed the A-beta fibers. The dorsal horn is sensitized. The pain is constant burning + paroxysmal stabbing, with allodynia in the affected dermatome. The dominant mechanism is calcium-channel mediated central sensitization. Gabapentin/pregabalin binds the alpha-2-delta subunit and reduces presynaptic neurotransmitter release in the dorsal horn.

## What You Missed (3 Gaps)

### 1. Treatment: Gabapentin/Pregabalin First-Line (Not Carbamazepine)

**Score impact: 66.67% on treatment (treated but suboptimal)**

You ordered medication, and it was accepted. But CCS deducted points because you did not order gabapentin/pregabalin first. Here's the reasoning:

This patient had a herpes zoster outbreak that injured CN V. The timeline (pain starting after shingles) makes this postherpetic neuralgia, not classical trigeminal neuralgia. The distinction matters because:

- Classical trigeminal neuralgia: vascular compression → focal demyelination → ephaptic cross-talk → carbamazepine
- Postherpetic neuralgia: viral ganglion destruction → A-beta fiber loss → central sensitization → gabapentin/pregabalin

**The treatment hierarchy for PHN:**
1. Gabapentin/pregabalin (first-line, alpha-2-delta calcium channel)
2. TCAs - amitriptyline, nortriptyline (second-line, SNRI + NMDA)
3. Antiseizure medications - valproic acid, carbamazepine (third-line, when above fail)

By ordering carbamazepine (or another anti-seizure medication that's not first-line for PHN), you got partial credit but lost points for not choosing the evidence-based first-line agent.

**The capsacin cream** prescribed by urgent care was also anterior treatment: topical capsaicin depletes substance P from C-fiber terminals, reducing nociceptive transmission. It's adjunctive, not primary therapy.

### 2. CBC with Differential

**Score impact: Diagnostic orders 42.86%**

The CCS algorithm wants you to rule out acute infection as a cause of symptoms. While shingles and PHN are post-infectious (not acute infection), CBC is a screening lab expected in nearly every CCS case. In postherpetic neuralgia specifically, you want to check for leukocytosis that might suggest ongoing active zoster rather than resolved PHN, or a secondary bacterial infection of the healed rash site.

### 3. BMP/CMP

**Score impact: Diagnostic orders 42.86%**

Chemistries are standard screening labs. In a patient with PHN being started on gabapentin (renally cleared) or TCAs (cardiotoxic in overdose, metabolized hepatically), baseline renal and hepatic function are prudent. Additionally, electrolyte abnormalities (particularly calcium, magnesium) can cause or exacerbate neuropathic symptoms.

## Attending's Summary

This is one of the most instructive cases you'll see. Two diseases in the same anatomical distribution, treated with opposite medications. The difference is mechanism.

Classical trigeminal neuralgia is a plumbing problem: an artery compresses the nerve, demyelination creates electrical cross-talk, sodium channels fire inappropriately. Carbamazepine shuts them down.

Postherpetic neuralgia is a destruction problem: the virus killed the A-beta gatekeepers, the dorsal horn is sensitized, calcium channels are driving excitatory neurotransmitter release. Gabapentin binds the calcium channel subunit and quiets the dorsal horn.

You recognized the neuralgia. Next time, ask: did shingles come before the pain? If yes, gabapentin. If no, and it's paroxysmal lightning pain, carbamazepine. That one question will save you the treatment points on both cases.
