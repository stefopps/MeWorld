## Mechanism: Why the Aorta Splits in Two

The aortic wall has three layers: intima (thin endothelial lining), media (thick smooth muscle and elastin), and adventitia (outer connective tissue). The media is the structural layer. Chronic hypertension damages it — smooth muscle cells degenerate, elastin fibers fragment, and the wall weakens.

A dissection begins with an **intimal tear.** Blood at systemic pressure (180 mmHg systolic in this patient) enters through the tear and splits the media longitudinally. This creates two lumens: the true lumen (original) and the false lumen (blood dissecting through the media).

The force driving the dissection forward is **dP/dt** — the rate of pressure rise in the left ventricle during systole. It's not just the absolute pressure. It's how fast the pressure hits the aortic wall. Each heartbeat is a hammer blow tearing the flap further down the aorta.

When the dissection involves the ascending aorta, it's **Type A (Stanford classification).** This is lethal for three reasons:

1. **Retrograde propagation into the coronary ostia** — the dissection flap covers the coronary artery openings → myocardial infarction (not plaque rupture, mechanical occlusion)
2. **Disruption of the aortic valve** — the flap undermines the commissures where the valve cusps attach → acute aortic regurgitation (the new diastolic murmur)
3. **Rupture into the pericardium** — blood dissects through the adventitia into the pericardial sac → cardiac tamponade → death

This patient already had aortic regurgitation. The valve was already involved. Tamponade was the next step.

## Spatial Logic: The Hemodynamic Sequence

The treatment of Type A dissection reverses the forces that tear the aorta.

**Step 1: Reduce dP/dt (beta-blocker FIRST).** IV esmolol or labetalol slows the heart rate and reduces contractile velocity. The aortic wall feels less shear stress with each beat. The dissection stops propagating. This must happen before vasodilation — if you vasodilate without beta-blockade, reflex tachycardia increases dP/dt and makes the dissection worse.

**Step 2: Reduce afterload (vasodilator SECOND).** Once the heart rate is controlled, IV nitroprusside or nicardipine drops the systolic blood pressure. Target SBP <120 mmHg. Lower pressure = lower wall tension = lower risk of rupture.

**Step 3: Control pain (IV opioids).** Pain triggers sympathetic activation → tachycardia and hypertension → increased dP/dt → the opposite of what you want. IV morphine or fentanyl breaks this cycle. The patient is nauseous — oral meds are not absorbed reliably.

**Step 4: Surgery (cardiothoracic, not vascular, not cardiology).** Type A = cardiothoracic surgery. Cardiopulmonary bypass. Aortic root repair or replacement. The surgeon resects the segment of aorta containing the intimal tear, obliterates the false lumen, and replaces it with a Dacron graft. If the aortic valve is involved, it gets repaired or replaced.

## Contrast: Type A vs Type B — the Only Question That Matters

| | Type A | Type B |
|---|---|---|
| **Location** | Ascending aorta (proximal to left subclavian) | Descending aorta only (distal to left subclavian) |
| **Treatment** | Emergency surgery — OR immediately | Medical management (BP control) unless complicated |
| **Mortality** | 1-2% per hour untreated | Lower acute mortality, but complications can require TEVAR |
| **Consultant** | Cardiothoracic surgery | Vascular surgery (for complicated) |

The Stanford classification is binary for a reason. All you need to know from the echo: is the ascending aorta involved? Yes = Type A = OR. No = Type B = medical ± TEVAR.

## Clinical Anchors

**Type A dissection = OR. No exceptions.** Don't manage a Type A dissection medically. Don't wait in the ICU. Don't get a second cardiology opinion. Get the surgeon.

**Beta-blocker before vasodilator. Always.** The reflex tachycardia from isolated vasodilation propagates the dissection. Esmolol or labetalol IV → then nitroprusside.

**Aspirin is contraindicated.** You avoided it. Good. Antiplatelets worsen surgical bleeding. This is not ACS.

**Pain control is hemodynamic control.** IV opioids reduce sympathetic tone. This is not comfort care — it's dissection management.

**CXR → CT/TEE → surgery.** That's the diagnostic pathway. You did CXR (widened mediastinum) → echo (confirmed type A with AR). The next step is the OR, not the ICU.

**ICU is a waiting room for a surgery you haven't ordered.** The patient deteriorated at 21:00 and again at 23:00 while in the ICU. He told you he was dying. He needed surgery, not monitoring.
