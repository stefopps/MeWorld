## Mechanism — How Dirty Needles Plant Bacteria on a Heart Valve

### The Infective Endocarditis Chain

1. **Endothelial injury opens the door.** IV drug use introduces particulate matter (talc, cotton fibers, bacteria) directly into venous circulation. These particles strike the endothelial surface of the tricuspid valve (right-sided) or — if there's a pre-existing abnormality — the mitral or aortic valve (left-sided). The endothelium is damaged. Platelets and fibrin deposit at the injury site. This is the **nonbacterial thrombotic endocarditis (NBTE)** — a sterile platelet-fibrin clot on a valve.

2. **Bacteremia seeds the clot.** With every injection, skin flora enter the bloodstream. *Staphylococcus aureus* is the most common organism in IVDU-associated endocarditis because it colonizes the skin and is highly virulent. The bacteria adhere to the platelet-fibrin clot via surface adhesins (MSCRAMMs — microbial surface components recognizing adhesive matrix molecules). They embed. They multiply. They form a **vegetation**: a biofilm of bacteria, platelets, fibrin, and inflammatory cells.

3. **The vegetation is a fortress.** Antibiotics cannot penetrate biofilm easily. The bacteria inside are metabolically inactive (persister cells), protected from both antibiotics and immune cells. This is why endocarditis requires **bactericidal** antibiotics for 4-6 weeks — not bacteriostatic agents — and sometimes valve surgery when antibiotics cannot clear the vegetation.

4. **Emboli shower the body.** Fragments of the vegetation break off and travel. Right-sided emboli → pulmonary septic emboli → lung abscesses. Left-sided emboli → brain (stroke, mycotic aneurysm), spleen (splenic abscess), kidneys (renal infarct), skin (Janeway lesions — septic microemboli in dermal vessels), retina (Roth spots).

5. **The murmur is the valve failing.** As the vegetation erodes the valve, the leaflets don't close properly. Mitral regurgitation produces a holosystolic murmur at the apex radiating to the axilla. The valve may perforate entirely.

### Antibiotics — The Right Drug for the Organism

| Organism | First-Line | Alternative |
|----------|-----------|-------------|
| **MSSA** (methicillin-sensitive S. aureus) | Nafcillin/oxacillin 2g IV q4h OR cefazolin | Vancomycin |
| **MRSA** (methicillin-resistant S. aureus) | **Vancomycin** IV OR **daptomycin** | Linezolid (bacteriostatic — not ideal) |
| *Streptococcus viridans* | Penicillin G OR ceftriaxone | Vancomycin |
| *Enterococcus* | Ampicillin + gentamicin | Vancomycin + gentamicin |
| *HACEK* organisms | Ceftriaxone | Ampicillin-sulbactam |

**For IVDU-associated endocarditis, empiric coverage MUST include MRSA until cultures return.** You cannot assume MSSA. You need vancomycin or daptomycin from the start.

### Why Aspirin Is Contraindicated

Aspirin does not prevent emboli in infective endocarditis. Multiple RCTs have shown no benefit. What aspirin does do: it increases the risk of hemorrhagic transformation of septic cerebral emboli. A vegetation fragment that causes a bland infarct becomes a hemorrhagic infarct on aspirin. This is a catastrophic outcome. **Aspirin has no role in acute infective endocarditis.**

## The Attending's Summary

You diagnosed endocarditis correctly. You ordered echo. You got blood cultures. You recognized the murmur and the skin findings. The problem is that you treated community-acquired pneumonia or strep bacteremia instead of IVDU-associated endocarditis.

**The three critical errors:**

1. **Penicillin does not cover MRSA.** This is a board-exam trap: MSSA endocarditis gets nafcillin. MRSA endocarditis gets vancomycin. When you don't know the organism and the patient uses IV drugs, you assume MRSA. Vancomycin is the only correct empiric choice.

2. **Aspirin is contraindicated in endocarditis.** It does not prevent emboli. It converts bland cerebral emboli into hemorrhagic strokes. This is a never-event.

3. **The case timed out because you spent too much time on diagnosis without starting treatment.** You ordered aspirin, culture, echo, CXR, preventive counseling — all reasonable — but you never hung the vancomycin. When you suspect IE, start empiric antibiotics immediately after drawing blood cultures. Do not wait for the echo result.

**Your mantra for the next febrile IVDU patient:** Blood cultures → vancomycin → echo → ID consult → social work. Five orders. In that sequence. Start the vancomycin within the first 15 minutes of simulated time.
