3x3 grid, 9 panels — the attending's diagnostic workup for diverticulitis, Part 1.

Story Spine: A 50-year-old man with LLQ pain, fever 38.2, HR 115. Abdominal XR looks normal. But CT abdomen/pelvis with contrast reveals a 2 cm pericolic abscess — the XR missed it completely. Blood cultures x2 are drawn before antibiotics — bacteria have translocated through the microperforated wall. UA excludes renal colic and pyelo — the inflamed sigmoid sits on the bladder causing sterile pyuria. Ciprofloxacin alone covers gram-negative aerobes only. The diverticulum is an anaerobic pocket. Bacteroides fragilis thrives inside. Metronidazole must be added. Both antibiotics = full coverage. Morphine treats the 9/10 pain.

Camera: split XR-vs-CT, macro cultures, macro UA, macro abscess, macro metronidazole, medium CMP, medium pain, macro NPO, wide fiber.

Panel 1 (ONCE — split, XR vs CT): Left: plain abdominal XR on lightbox — gas pattern normal, no free air, no obstruction. Limited information. Right: CT cross-section on monitor — same patient. Sigmoid colon thickened wall, pericolic fat stranding, a 2 cm rim-enhancing fluid collection. The abscess. XR missed it. CT is the gold standard. The single most important order.

Panel 2 (EVERY DAY — macro, blood cultures): Two blood culture bottles — aerobic and anaerobic — under lab light. Gloved hand drawing blood from antecubital vein. T 38.2, HR 115. Bacteria translocated through the microperforated diverticular wall into mesenteric veins, then systemic circulation. Culture before antibiotics. Identify the organism before you sterilize the blood.

Panel 3 (UNTIL — macro, UA): Urine dipstick and culture plate on lab bench under light. Inflamed sigmoid colon visible behind the bladder — sitting directly on the bladder dome. Leukocytes in the urine from direct bladder irritation by the inflamed colon. Sterile pyuria. NOT a UTI. UA distinguishes diverticulitis from pyelonephritis. Two diseases, same quadrant, same presentation.

Panel 4 (BECAUSE — macro, abscess aerobes vs anaerobes): Pericolic abscess at microscopic scale. Left half: gram-negative rods multiplying on the surface — ciprofloxacin molecules binding DNA gyrase, killing them. Right half: Bacteroides fragilis thriving deep in the abscess core where oxygen is depleted — ciprofloxacin molecules bouncing off, completely ineffective. One antibiotic = half the coverage. The abscess is a polymicrobial pocket.

Panel 5 (THEREFORE — macro, metronidazole): Same abscess, metronidazole now added. Right half: metronidazole molecules penetrating the anaerobe cell wall. Reduced by bacterial nitroreductase into cytotoxic radicals. DNA strands snapping apart. Bacteroides fragilis dying. Both gram-negative aerobes and anaerobes now covered. The abscess clearing.

Panel 6 (BUT — medium, CMP): Chemistry panel results under lab light. Electrolytes: sodium, potassium, creatinine, BUN. Renal function determines antibiotic dosing. Ciprofloxacin is renally excreted — dose must be adjusted for elevated Cr. Nausea and vomiting mean the patient is volume depleted. Check renal function before starting nephrotoxic drugs.

Panel 7 (THEREFORE — medium, pain): IV line in antecubital fossa. Morphine vial drawn into syringe under warm golden light. 9/10 pain on the patient's face behind. Pain drives sympathetic surge: tachycardia, hypertension, splanchnic vasoconstriction reducing gut perfusion. Treating pain is not masking — it is reducing physiologic stress that worsens the inflammatory cascade.

Panel 8 (UNTIL — macro, NPO): Patient's mouth closed. Nothing by mouth. Sigmoid colon visualized behind — resting, no peristaltic waves propagating. The sigmoid generates pressures up to 90 mmHg during peristalsis — the highest segmental pressure in the entire GI tract. Every wave drives more bacteria through the inflamed, microperforated wall. NPO silences the mechanical pump feeding the infection.

Panel 9 (EVER SINCE — wide, fiber): Kitchen table spread with high fiber foods — whole grains, legumes, leafy vegetables, fresh fruit. Same sigmoid behind — larger soft stool distending the lumen evenly. Intraluminal pressure low. No herniation through the muscularis wall. Decades of low fiber created the diverticula. Decades of high fiber prevent the next episode. The lifelong prevention lock.
