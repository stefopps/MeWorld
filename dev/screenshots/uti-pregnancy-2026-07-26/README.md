## Hook

A pregnant woman with a simple UTI. Urine culture shows E. coli sensitive to ciprofloxacin. You see the sensitivity report and reflexively reach for the fluoroquinolone. The case hits you with 0% on treatment.

**Fluoroquinolones are contraindicated in pregnancy.** The mechanism is not subtle: they chelate magnesium in developing cartilage, disrupt chondrocyte DNA gyrase, and cause irreversible damage to fetal joint development. The culture says "sensitive." The pregnancy says "no."

---

## Mechanism — Why Fluoroquinolones Are Teratogenic to Cartilage

Fluoroquinolones (ciprofloxacin, levofloxacin, moxifloxacin) work by inhibiting **DNA gyrase** and **topoisomerase IV** — bacterial enzymes that manage DNA supercoiling during replication. The problem is that these enzymes exist in human cells too, especially in rapidly dividing tissues.

**Fetal cartilage:** Developing chondrocytes are actively dividing throughout gestation. Fluoroquinolones penetrate the placenta, concentrate in fetal cartilage, and:

1. **Inhibit mitochondrial DNA replication** in chondrocytes → apoptosis of cartilage precursor cells
2. **Chelate magnesium ions** — Mg2+ is a cofactor for integrin-mediated chondrocyte adhesion to extracellular matrix. Chelation disrupts normal cartilage architecture
3. **Generate reactive oxygen species** → oxidative damage to developing joint surfaces

The result in animal studies: irreversible arthropathy, erosion of weight-bearing joints, and lameness. Human data is limited (unethical to randomize pregnant women to fluoroquinolones), but the animal evidence is so strong that fluoroquinolones are **FDA Pregnancy Category C** (animal harm demonstrated) and are avoided in pregnancy by universal consensus.

**This is why it does not matter that the culture says "sensitive."** The drug works on the bacteria but destroys the baby's joints. You must choose from the pregnancy-safe alternatives.

## Pregnancy-Safe UTI Antibiotics

| Antibiotic | Trimester | Duration | Mechanism | Pearl |
|-----------|-----------|----------|-----------|-------|
| **Nitrofurantoin** | 2nd & 3rd only | 5 days | Reduced by bacterial flavoproteins → reactive intermediates damage ribosomal proteins, DNA, cell wall | Avoid 1st trimester (cardiac defects). Avoid near delivery (hemolytic anemia in G6PD-deficient newborns) |
| **Cephalexin** | All | 3 days | Beta-lactam — inhibits cell wall transpeptidase | Culture showed resistance in this case (cephalosporin-resistant E. coli) |
| **Amoxicillin-clavulanate** | All | 3 days | Beta-lactam + beta-lactamase inhibitor | Broad-spectrum; clavulanate overcomes resistance |
| **Fosfomycin** | All | Single 3g dose | Inhibits MurA (UDP-GlcNAc enolpyruvyl transferase) — first step of peptidoglycan synthesis | Simplest regimen; good for non-adherent patients |

**For this specific case:** The E. coli was resistant to ALL cephalosporins (ampicillin, cephalothin, cefixime, ceftriaxone, ceftizoxime). That eliminates cephalexin. Best choice: **nitrofurantoin x 5 days** (second trimester = safe window) or **fosfomycin single dose**.

## TMP-SMx — The Other Contraindicated Drug in Pregnancy

Trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole (Bactrim) has different toxicity profiles by trimester:

| Trimester | Risk | Mechanism |
|-----------|------|-----------|
| **First** | Neural tube defects | Trimethoprim is a folate antagonist — inhibits dihydrofolate reductase (DHFR). Neural tube closure depends on folate-dependent methylation. |
| **Third** | Kernicterus | Sulfamethoxazole crosses placenta, displaces bilirubin from albumin binding sites. Free unconjugated bilirubin crosses neonatal blood-brain barrier → basal ganglia deposition. |
| **All** | High resistance | E. coli resistance to TMP-SMx is common. Safer alternatives exist. |

## Why Pregnancy = Complicated UTI (Always)

UTIs are classified as "complicated" if any of these are true:
- Male sex
- Pregnancy
- Structural/neurologic abnormality
- Immunosuppression
- Upper tract involvement (pyelonephritis)
- Indwelling catheter

**Pregnancy alone makes it complicated** because:
1. **Progesterone** relaxes ureteral smooth muscle → ureteral dilation → urinary stasis
2. **Mechanical compression** of the gravid uterus on the bladder → incomplete emptying
3. **Increased GFR** → more glucose and amino acids in urine → bacterial growth medium
4. **Asymptomatic bacteriuria** progresses to pyelonephritis in 25-30% of untreated pregnant women (vs 1-2% in non-pregnant)

This is why **even asymptomatic bacteriuria is treated in pregnancy.** Two consecutive clean-catch specimens with the same organism = treat with a pregnancy-safe antibiotic.

## Key Takeaway

**Fluoroquinolones and pregnancy do not mix.** When a culture comes back sensitive to ciprofloxacin in a pregnant patient, you mentally cross it off the list and pick from the pregnancy-safe menu: nitrofurantoin (2nd/3rd trimester), cephalexin, amoxicillin-clavulanate, or fosfomycin.

**The pattern:** CCS will give you a culture with a tempting sensitivity result that is contraindicated by the patient's clinical context. The test is whether you read the sensitivity panel or the patient. Always read the patient.
