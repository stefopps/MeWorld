# Crohn's Disease with Perianal Skin Tags — Attending Walkthrough

**Date:** 2026-07-23 | **Score:** 73.3% | **Patient:** 30-year-old male, forest ranger

---

## Hook

He's 30. He's been losing weight for 2 months — 10 pounds. He has loose stools, crampy abdominal pain, and a perianal pain he's embarrassed to talk about.

You do the rectal exam. You find perianal skin tags and guaiac-positive stool.

This is not hemorrhoids. This is not IBS. Skin tags in a young adult with chronic diarrhea are Crohn's disease until the colonoscopy proves otherwise.

---

## Mechanism: Transmural Inflammation

Crohn's is not a mucosal disease. It's transmural. The inflammation goes all the way through the bowel wall, from mucosa to serosa. That's why the complications are different from ulcerative colitis.

**Fistulas happen because the inflammation tunnels through.** The inflamed terminal ileum sits next to the bladder, the sigmoid, the psoas muscle. The inflammatory process bores through the muscularis propria, creates a tract, and connects two epithelial surfaces that were never meant to touch. Entero-enteric, enterovesical, enterocutaneous — these are just descriptions of where the tunnel surfaced.

**Strictures happen because the transmural inflammation heals with fibrosis.** Every flare lays down collagen. Over years, the lumen narrows. Not from active inflammation — from scar. Anti-inflammatory drugs won't open a fibrotic stricture. That's why you screen with imaging, not just scopes.

**Skip lesions happen because Crohn's is segmental.** The inflammation doesn't flow continuously along the bowel like UC does. It skips. Normal mucosa sits right next to ulcerated mucosa. This is the hallmark that separates Crohn's from UC on endoscopy.

---

## Spatial Logic: The Terminal Ileum

The terminal ileum is ground zero for Crohn's in 70% of cases. Why?

The ileocecal valve is the choke point. Luminal contents — bacteria, dietary antigens, bile acids — stall here before crossing into the colon. The immune system of the gut-associated lymphoid tissue (GALT) is concentrated in the terminal ileum. In a genetically susceptible host, the immune response to those stalled antigens goes wrong. It doesn't turn off.

The terminal ileum also happens to be where vitamin B12 and bile acids are absorbed. And — critically — where iron is absorbed. Normal hemoglobin does not rule out iron deficiency here. The ferritin drops long before the Hgb. If you don't check iron studies, you discharge a patient who is quietly becoming anemic.

---

## Contrast: Crohn's vs Ulcerative Colitis

**Crohn's:** Transmural. Skip lesions. Terminal ileum. Right colon. Perianal disease (skin tags, fistulas, abscesses). Cobblestone mucosa. Fistulas and strictures. Smoking makes it worse.

**Ulcerative Colitis:** Mucosal only. Continuous, starts at rectum and moves proximally. No skip lesions. Left colon predominance. Bloody diarrhea is the hallmark. No perianal disease. Smoking is paradoxically protective.

The skin tags on rectal exam are the quickest way to separate them at the bedside. UC doesn't do perianal tags. Crohn's does. They're not hemorrhoids — they're inflammatory tags from chronic perianal disease.

---

## Clinical Anchors

**Colonoscopy is the diagnostic gold standard.** Skip lesions + cobblestoning + terminal ileal involvement + rectal sparing = Crohn's. Biopsy shows transmural inflammation with non-caseating granulomas in 30-40%.

**Budesonide is first-line for mild-moderate ileocecal Crohn's.** It's a topical steroid with high first-pass metabolism — it hits the gut and gets cleared by the liver before it causes systemic steroid side effects. Prednisone is reserved for more severe disease.

**Mesalamine has limited evidence in Crohn's.** It's a UC drug. It works on the mucosa, and Crohn's is transmural. Mesalamine can't reach the deep inflammation. Biologics (anti-TNF) are disease-modifying for moderate-severe Crohn's.

**Colon cancer screening is intensified.** His father died of colon cancer. He has Crohn's colitis. Both raise his risk. He needs surveillance colonoscopy sooner and more frequently than average-risk screening.

## What You Missed

| Missed | Impact |
|--------|--------|
| **Iron + TIBC** | Terminal ileum absorbs iron — ferritin drops before Hgb |
| **Vitamin B12** | Ileal disease impairs B12 absorption |
| **Folate** | Chronic inflammation + poor intake |
| **CRP / ESR** | Baseline inflammation markers |
| **C. diff PCR** | Ruled out infectious etiology |
| **Smoking cessation** | Smoking worsens Crohn's — modifiable risk factor |
