## THE CRITICAL ERROR

Ordered: **Ceftriaxone IV continuous** (Day 1 @ 9:18)
Should have ordered: **Oral vancomycin 125 mg PO QID x 10 days**

Ceftriaxone is a 3rd-generation cephalosporin. Cephalosporins are a KNOWN CAUSE of C. difficile — they kill normal gut flora and let C. diff spores germinate unchecked. You confirmed C. diff by PCR at Day 1 @ 10:15, then left the ceftriaxone running. The patient decompensated over 2 days because the antibiotic making the infection worse was never stopped.

---

## The Red Man Syndrome Mnemonic

Vancomycin given intravenously causes Red Man Syndrome — histamine release from mast cells produces flushing, erythema, and pruritus. It's NOT an allergic reaction. It's a rate-dependent infusion reaction. Slow the drip.

Oral vancomycin does NOT cause Red Man Syndrome. The vancomycin molecule (~1,449 Da) is too large to pass through intestinal tight junctions. It stays in the gut lumen where C. diff lives. Zero systemic absorption = zero Red Man Syndrome.

The visual signal: patient's skin is red in the hospital bed. This is the signal to save vancomycin for the gut — not the bloodstream.

---

## C. diff Treatment Algorithm (IDSA 2021)

Non-severe (WBC <15K AND Cr <1.5):
→ Oral vancomycin 125 mg PO QID x 10 days
→ OR Oral fidaxomicin 200 mg PO BID x 10 days

Severe (WBC >=15K OR Cr >=1.5):
→ Oral vancomycin 125 mg PO QID x 10 days

Fulminant (hypotension, shock, ileus, megacolon):
→ Oral vancomycin 500 mg PO QID + IV metronidazole + surgical consult

Metronidazole alone is no longer first-line in ANY severity tier (guidelines changed 2017-2021).

---

## Pathophysiology

1. Trigger: Broad-spectrum antibiotics kill normal gut flora
2. C. diff spores survive (acid-resistant) and germinate without competition
3. Toxin A (enterotoxin): fluid secretion + mucosal damage
4. Toxin B (cytotoxin): 10x more potent, glucosylates Rho GTPases → actin depolymerization → tight junction breakdown
5. Pseudomembrane: yellow-white plaques of fibrin, neutrophils, necrotic debris
6. Oral vancomycin: too large to cross gut wall, stays in lumen, kills C. diff locally
