α, β, Power & p-value — the four players in every hypothesis test

α (red) is set before data — your false-positive budget. p-value (red ribbon) is computed after — how extreme your actual result was. β (amber) is the false-negative zone under the alternative. Power = 1−β (green) is your ability to catch a real effect. Every slider starts at zero — build up from the base.

p-value, β, & Power

Red = p-value (area under null right of zobs). Amber = β (false neg under H₁). Green = power = 1−β (true pos under H₁).

Start here: All three sliders are at zero. There is no effect (d=0), no observation yet (zobs=0). Alpha is at 5% — the standard default. Pull each slider one at a time to watch what changes.

Observed z (zobs): The test statistic from your actual trial data. If your sample mean converts to a z-score of 2.10, that's your zobs. The p-value is the area under the null to the right of this point — "how unusual would this result be IF the drug did nothing?"
Effect d (Cohen's d): How far apart the two distributions are, measured in standard deviation units. d=0 means no effect (identical). d=0.2 is small, 0.5 medium, 0.8 large. Max here is d=2.0 — a huge effect where the distributions barely touch.
