Question 65 · Central Tendency & Outliers

Which measure of central tendency is MOST affected by these two extreme values?

A researcher surveys 100 patients with osteoarthritis about their pain scores (range 0–100). 98 patients cluster between 28–48, peaking around 32. Two patients score 100. Every single dot below is its own patient and can be dragged anywhere along the line — the two darker dots are the original outliers, but grab any of the 100 and watch how differently the mean, median, and mode respond.

Mean
37.7
Median
35
Mode
32
AMode — the most frequent value shifts dramatically when extreme outliers appear.
Incorrect. The mode barely changes — it's still wherever the data is densest. Drag the outliers around and watch: the mode doesn't move.
BMean — the arithmetic average is pulled toward the extreme values.
Correct. Drag an outlier further out and the mean keeps following it, every time, by exactly (change \u00f7 100).
CMedian — the middle value shifts because the extreme values change the ordering.
Incorrect. Drag an outlier from 100 down to 60, then down to 51 - the median barely twitches, because only its rank matters, not how far out it sits.
DAll three are equally affected — outliers change the entire distribution.
Incorrect. Drag an outlier and compare how far each of the three markers actually travels - they are not remotely equal.
ENone is affected — measures of central tendency are robust to outliers by definition.
Incorrect. Watch the mean marker while you drag - it moves every single time. Only the median and mode are resistant.
Mnemonic: the mean is a weakling — every data point pulls on it. The median is a rank-based wall — only position matters, not magnitude. The mode is a crowd — it only cares about who shows up the most.
